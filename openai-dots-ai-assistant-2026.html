OpenAI Dots: What They Are and How They Work in 2026

OpenAI is rolling out a new type of AI agent called a dot. Unlike a normal chatbot conversation, a dot is designed to take ongoing responsibility for work and continue making progress between conversations.

Quick answer: An OpenAI dot is an always-on AI agent inside ChatGPT. It is powered by GPT-6 Astra, has its own cloud computer and can work with apps you choose to connect.

What is an OpenAI dot?

A dot is a persistent AI agent designed to handle ongoing work instead of only responding to individual prompts.

You can give your dot a goal, provide instructions and decide what it is allowed to do. It can then work through tasks and bring results back for you to review.

How is a dot different from regular ChatGPT?

A regular ChatGPT conversation generally focuses on the requests you make during a conversation.

A dot can take on continuing responsibility. OpenAI says dots can keep making progress between conversations, remember relevant context and determine what needs to happen next while still turning to you for decisions that require your judgment.

Does a dot have its own computer?

Yes. Each dot has its own cloud computer.

This allows the agent to work with websites, software and connected apps without needing to constantly use your personal computer.

Access to your local computer is optional and starts turned off.

What can OpenAI Dots do?

OpenAI says users can give dots tasks such as reviewing a calendar, researching topics, handling recurring work and following up on ongoing responsibilities.

Dots can also work across apps that you choose to connect, giving them the ability to help with workflows that involve more than one service.

Could small businesses use Dots?

Potentially. The always-on design could make dots useful for business tasks that happen repeatedly rather than only once.

Examples could include researching competitors, preparing recurring reports, organizing information or helping coordinate work across connected business apps.

The exact usefulness will depend on which apps a business connects, what permissions it provides and how the dot is instructed.

Are OpenAI Dots fully autonomous?

Dots are designed to work independently on authorized tasks, but that does not mean users should give an AI agent unlimited control.

OpenAI provides controls for defining what a dot can do, and the agent can return to the user when a decision requires human judgment.

Businesses should review permissions carefully and keep human oversight for important financial, legal, customer or operational decisions.

Who can use OpenAI Dots?

OpenAI says dots are rolling out to eligible ChatGPT Pro users in supported markets and are also available to Business Premium users. Enterprise users can access the beta when enabled by their workspace administrator.

Availability may change as OpenAI expands the product, so users should check their current ChatGPT plan for access.

Why do OpenAI Dots matter?

Dots represent a larger shift in AI from assistants that wait for prompts toward agents that can take responsibility for ongoing work.

For entrepreneurs and small businesses, that could eventually mean delegating repetitive digital work to an AI agent while keeping control over important decisions.

OpenAI Dots vs AI automation

Traditional automation usually follows rules that someone sets up ahead of time. An AI agent can potentially reason through a task, adapt when circumstances change and decide which authorized step should happen next.

That makes dots particularly interesting for work that is too flexible or complicated for a simple automated rule.

Final thoughts

OpenAI Dots could be an important step toward AI agents that function more like ongoing digital assistants than traditional chatbots.

The biggest difference is persistence: instead of asking AI to help with every individual step, users can give an agent an ongoing responsibility and have it continue working within the permissions they provide.

For small businesses, the most interesting question may be how much repetitive work these agents can eventually handle safely while business owners focus on decisions that still require human judgment.
